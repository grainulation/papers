\section{Background}
\label[appendix]{app:background}

\paragraph{Staged compilation.} LLVM separates compilation into front end, optimization passes over a shared IR, and back end \citep{lattner2004llvm}. The design adopts the same shape: agent outputs are source languages, the claim IR is the shared representation, and passes perform validation, conflict detection, and selection. As in compilers, a well-chosen IR makes the downstream passes simple. Here, typed triples make conflict detection a slot lookup.

\paragraph{Consensus and replicated data.} Paxos \citep{lamport1998parttime} and Raft \citep{ongaro2014raft} achieve agreement among crash-prone processes, and PBFT \citep{castro1999pbft} tolerates $f$ Byzantine faults with $3f + 1$ replicas. Our problem is different: agent failures are epistemic (wrong, stale, or miscalibrated claims) rather than crashes or protocol deviations, and the reducer decides once over a closed set rather than agreeing over time. From conflict-free replicated data types \citep{shapiro2011crdt} the reducer borrows idempotent merge: re-submitting an identical contribution yields the same content-addressed identifier, and validation removes the duplicate.

\paragraph{Ensembles and frameworks.} \citet{dietterich2000ensemble} gives statistical, computational, and representational reasons why ensembles outperform their members. Our design draws on the intuition but combines discrete claims under an explicit, recorded rule rather than averaging predictions. Orchestration frameworks for LLM agents route messages and tools between agents but leave the final decision to a model. Learned rankers likewise combine many signals through functions that are opaque at decision time. The reducer's distinguishing property is that each decision carries a per-feature justification that can be recomputed.

\paragraph{Voting theory.} \citet{arrow1951social} shows that no ranked voting rule over three or more alternatives satisfies unrestricted domain, non-dictatorship, Pareto efficiency, and independence of irrelevant alternatives together. \citet{condorcet1785essai} introduced pairwise majority comparison and the jury theorem: if voters are independently more likely than not to be right, majority accuracy approaches one as their number grows. The independence assumption is exactly what correlated LLM errors violate. Schulze's method \citep{schulze2011new} is monotonic, clone-independent, and Condorcet-consistent. The reducer uses it as a tie-breaker over features rather than over agents, which \cref{sec:freetext} shows lets an unweighted feature vote.
