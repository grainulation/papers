\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\emph{Aggregation.} Self-consistency votes over sampled reasoning paths \citep{wang2023selfconsistency}, and debate has agents revise answers after reading each other \citep{du2024debate}. \citet{kaesberg2025voting} find voting stronger than consensus on reasoning tasks. Recent studies find that majority voting accounts for most of debate's gains \citep{choi2025debate,zhang2025stop} and improve on it with confidence- and diversity-aware debate \citep{zhu2026demystifying} or second-order aggregation across models \citep{ai2026beyond}. \citet{cemri2025why} catalog 14 failure modes of multi-agent systems. These works improve the accuracy of the combined decision. We focus on recording the decision so that it can be audited. OVERTURN agrees that majority vote is a strong baseline once answers are constrained, and shows a deterministic way past it: overturning only through verified answers corrects 61\% of wrong majorities. \citet{zheng2025byzantine} study multi-agent reliability through Byzantine fault tolerance; we target auditability of the decision rather than consensus under Byzantine faults.

\emph{LLMs as judges.} Model judges track human preferences but show position, verbosity, and self-enhancement biases \citep{zheng2023judging}. Later work quantifies a dozen bias types \citep{ye2024justice}, shows position bias varies with judge, task, and candidate quality gap \citep{shi2025judging}, and surveys reliability concerns \citep{gu2024survey}. Our design confines model judgment to the narrowest task, labeling one passage against one proposition, where it can be validated in advance. The label is sealed, the checker's identity is hashed into the policy, and the gate ties every loss of a correct default to a verified wrong answer that can be inspected. On OVERTURN this recovers two thirds of a Sonnet judge's gain over the vote while every decision stays a replayable function.

\emph{Determinism.} Nominally deterministic LLM settings still vary across runs \citep{atil2025nondeterminism}, and results shift with batch size, GPU count, and precision because floating-point addition is non-associative \citep{yuan2025numerical}. Batch-invariant kernels remove much of this at the serving layer \citep{he2025nondeterminism}. Our approach complements this with the purely functional deployment model of Nix \citep{dolstra2004nix}: record non-deterministic outputs as content-addressed inputs and make the downstream function pure. Exact logging with any deterministic reducer would also replay (\suppref{S9}); sealing adds typed inputs, tamper evidence, and conditions for justified selection.

\emph{Claim extraction and verification.} \citet{ullrich2025claim} benchmark claim extraction for fact-checking, and \citet{kolli2025hybrid} verify claims with knowledge graphs, LLMs, and search. Either kind of system can serve as an artifact producer at the arbitration boundary, for lowering or for support checking, and their benchmarks are the natural yardstick for our rule-based lowering (\suppref{S3}). Verify-then-vote also applies retrieval-grounded verification of the kind FEVER and SciFact pioneered \citep{thorne2018fever,wadden2020scifact} to aggregation: the verifier licenses an overturn rather than producing the answer.

\emph{Knowledge conflicts.} Work on conflicts between a model's parametric knowledge and retrieved context \citep{xu2024knowledge} finds that models adopt incorrect retrieved content readily, more so the less confident their prior \citep{wu2024clasheval}. Verify-then-vote takes the conflict out of the model: agents may disagree with each other and with the corpus, and only a checked label on a cited passage can move the decision. False sources are the residue of this conflict that a correct checker cannot remove, and \cref{prop:gate-harm} counts them.

\emph{Verifiers, attribution, and crowd aggregation.} Verifier-guided selection reranks sampled solutions with a trained outcome or process verifier \citep{cobbe2021verifiers,lightman2024verify}. Verify-then-vote keeps the vote as the default and lets a verifier act only through a cited passage, so each overturn can be checked against sealed text. Attribution work defines and measures whether generated text is supported by its cited source \citep{rashkin2023ais,gao2023alce}, and compact grounding checkers \citep{tang2024minicheck} are candidates for a local checker once they label refutation as well as support under our unchanged claim representation (\suppref{S8}). Classical label aggregation weights annotators by estimated error rates \citep{dawid1979maximum}, and the surprisingly-popular rule overturns a majority using respondents' predictions of others' answers \citep{prelec2017solution}. In verify-then-vote, the overturn signal is external evidence, checked once and sealed.

\subsection{Scope}
\label{sec:limitations}

The results hold under the following conditions. Accuracy gains require agents that cite passages the checker can read. On OVERTURN these come from a fixed corpus. Open web citations resolve and attach to their claims far less often (\suppref{S6}), which limits what the checker can verify. The harm bound is exact for the rule's deterministic default and states harm in units of verified wrong answers. Its practical value is therefore set jointly by the checker's error rate, entity resolution, the correctness of the corpus, and, in evaluation, the completeness of the gold. The checker, an LLM, is part of the trusted computing base. The classification of OVERTURN's verified wrong answers rests on two same-family model annotators and model adjudication, without independent human validation; their agreement does not establish attribution accuracy. Verified default first was specified after the first pool's analysis and is tested on one further pool using the same questions and partly reused checker labels, so this is not independent confirmation on unseen questions. The pooled sensitivity analysis allows dependence between pools but still relies on a binomial model within each pool; it does not establish harm below the pre-specified ceiling (\suppref{S8.3}). TruthfulQA non-inferiority was established on the first pool and remains inconclusive on the second. The historical harm forecast uses gold-incorrect-answer exposure rather than adjudicated non-entailing passages and omits the false-refute validation channel. It is not a calibrated estimate of checker-caused harm; model-based attribution cannot repair those definition gaps. The passage ablation demonstrates dependence on supplied text, not the absence of parametric-knowledge influence. Tamper evidence is relative to a certificate the verifier holds independently of the bundle and assumes no relevant hash collision. The reference certificate is only 64 bits (\suppref{S2}); the tests do not establish cryptographic security against an adversary searching for collisions. The benchmark uses two pools of five open-weight models, one checker, one seed, and one machine. The cross-build replay matrix ran on one physical host with both x86-64 builds binary-translated, demonstrating replay on the tested instruction sets, operating systems, and C libraries. Captures attest the bytes received rather than origin integrity. Conflict detection follows declared predicate arity and exclusivity, and quantifier scope, temporal qualification, and complex negation lie outside the lookup. The protocol was frozen and hash-committed before data collection, but no public registry timestamp exists, so its timing rests on the study records. A \emph{verified} label also means only that the checker accepted the cited passage as supporting the answer; it can mislead if the checker or the passage is wrong, so readers should not treat it as proof the answer is true.

\subsection{Future work}
\label{sec:future}

Natural next steps are human-validated reading of the verified wrong answers and of the gold, and a local three-way checker validated to the hosted checker's thresholds, which would make every decision free of hosted calls. Others are verify-then-vote over open web evidence and replay of live-ingestion histories with streaming retraction.
