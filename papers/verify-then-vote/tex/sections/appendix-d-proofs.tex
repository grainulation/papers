Outside the formalization: floating-point error before quantization (the model uses exact arithmetic), Schulze itself (\cref{lem:neutrality} holds for any selector returning a member of its input), and the remark that false-positive counts are nonlinear in the weights.

\paragraph{Policy hash.} SHA-256, truncated to 128 bits, over a canonical JSON object with two sections, sorted by key and written without whitespace: \code{config} (every configurable field, including the exclusivity table with each group's objects sorted and de-duplicated) and \code{constants} (score scale, lowering factors, evidence-type weights, verification table, and the sorted functional predicates). Floats use shortest round-trip decimal form; non-finite floats are rejected. Optional fields are serialized only when they differ from their defaults, so setting any of them changes the policy hash and therefore the certificate.

\paragraph{Root hash and certificate.} The root hash is SHA-256, truncated to 128 bits, over the canonical serialization of $A$, including the identifier-scheme tag. The certificate is SHA-256, truncated to 64 bits, over the root hash, the policy hash, and the admitted identifiers in admission order.
